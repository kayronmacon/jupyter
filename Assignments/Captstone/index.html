Network Intrusion Detection

A machine learning pipeline to classify network traffic as benign or one of five cyberattack categories, modeled after the CICIDS 2017 dataset.

7 guided sections

Notebook Sections

1

Problem Understanding

Define the analytical goal, target variable, and real-world relevance of ML-based intrusion detection.

2

Data Exploration

Load dataset, inspect shape and types, visualize class distribution, and analyze missing values.

3

Data Cleaning & Preparation

Remove duplicates, cap outliers at the 99th percentile, fix negative IAT values, and impute with median.

4

Feature Engineering

Create Total Packets, Bytes per Packet, and Packet Asymmetry features; encode labels; scale features.

5

Modeling & Evaluation

Train Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting on an 80/20 stratified split.

6

Insight Generation

Per-class precision, recall, and F1 analysis; SOC triage recommendations based on model confidence.

7

Limitations & Future Work

Identifies class imbalance, synthetic data constraints, and paths forward with SMOTE and deep learning.

Models Compared

Model Strengths Split
Logistic Regression Fast, interpretable linear baseline 80 / 20 stratified
Random Forest Top Robust to outliers; provides feature importance 80 / 20 stratified
Gradient Boosting Learns complex non-linear boundaries 80 / 20 stratified

Key Findings

DDoS & Port Scan

High F1 scores due to distinct traffic patterns — extreme byte rates and very short flow durations make these classes easy to separate.

Minority Classes

Botnet and WebAttack show lower recall due to class imbalance. SMOTE or class-weight tuning are recommended next steps.

SOC Application

High-confidence DDoS and PortScan predictions can be auto-triaged; ambiguous predictions routed to human analysts.
